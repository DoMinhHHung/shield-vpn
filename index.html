Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers to the most common questions about Virtual Private Networks.

Yes, in the vast majority of countries (including the US, UK, Canada, and most of Europe), using a VPN is entirely legal. They are standard security tools used by businesses and individuals. However, performing illegal activities while connected to a VPN remains illegal. A few countries, such as China, Russia, and Iran, heavily restrict or ban non-government-approved VPNs.
Because a VPN routes your traffic through an encrypted server, there is usually a slight drop in speed. However, premium VPNs using modern protocols like WireGuard have reduced this speed loss to barely noticeable levels (often retaining 90%+ of your original speed). In some cases, if your ISP is throttling your connection, a VPN can actually improve your speed.
A no-logs (or zero-logs) policy is a guarantee from a VPN provider that they do not collect or store data about your online activities, connection timestamps, or original IP address. The best VPNs have their no-logs policies verified by independent auditing firms to ensure they are true to their word.
While there are a few safe free VPNs (usually limited versions of premium services), most completely free VPNs on app stores should be avoided. They often sustain their business by logging and selling your data, injecting ads, or offering weak encryption. If privacy is your goal, a premium vetted VPN is highly recommended.
Setting up a modern VPN is very straightforward. You simply sign up for a service, download their dedicated app for your device (Windows, Mac, iOS, Android), log in, and click "Connect". The app automatically handles the complex encryption and server routing in the background.
